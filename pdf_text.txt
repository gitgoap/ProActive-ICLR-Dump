1. Executive Summary
The strongest trajectory is Active Diagnostic Sets for Multimodal Reliability Under Probe Cost. The paper studies a new 
evaluation-and-learning object: given a multimodal prediction, which diagnostic evidence should an evaluator acquire 
before deciding whether the prediction is visually fragile, language-prior driven, alignment-sensitive, mixed, unclear, or safe  
enough to leave unchanged?
The main intellectual move is to replace point-source diagnosis with calibrated diagnostic sets. This matches the empirical 
reality that multimodal failures are often entangled. A system should not always output one confident source label. It should  
instead acquire evidence until it can return a small set of plausible failure sources with coverage, or explicitly retain a larger  
set when evidence is insufficient.
The paper should be positioned at the intersection of multimodal hallucination diagnosis, active feature acquisition, 
adaptive test-time compute, and conformal/risk-controlled prediction. This is the key to making it an ICLR-shaped paper 
rather than a follow-up evaluation paper.
ICLR target claim. Active diagnostic acquisition recovers most of the full-probe diagnostic value at far lower average 
forward-pass cost, yields smaller calibrated diagnostic sets than clean-only or fixed-probe baselines, generalizes across 
held-out model families, and supports break-aware correction decisions without claiming universal mitigation.
The full probe suite should remain an audit-time teacher and upper-bound diagnostic reference. The follow-up must not say  
that full probing is useless. It should say that full probing defines the evidence space, while the new method learns which 
evidence is needed for each instance.
2. Final Problem Statement
We study multimodal uncertainty as active diagnostic measurement. A Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) 
produces an answer for an image-prompt pair. The evaluator has access to cheap clean-pass features such as scalar 
confidence, answer length, entropy or margin, question type, image-quality measures, and optional hidden/logit features 
when available. It can then choose costly diagnostic probes. Each probe perturbs the input or asks a targeted re-check, 
producing answer flips, confidence shifts, and match signals.
The goal is to learn a policy that decides which evidence to acquire, when to stop, and what diagnostic set to output. The 
output is not a single causal source. It is a calibrated set of plausible failure states. This is crucial because a visually fragile 
answer can also be language-prior sensitive or alignment-sensitive.
Instance: x = (I, q, y_hat, M), where I is the image, q is the prompt, y_hat is the model answer, and M is the evaluated 
MLLM.
Probe actions: Stop, Visual, Blank, Grounding, and Relation. Visual probes degrade the image. Blank probes remove 
image evidence. Grounding probes ask the model to re-check the image. Relation probes swap or stress explicit spatial or 
semantic relations when available.
Observation from a probe: o_a(x) contains the perturbed answer, whether the answer flips, the confidence shift, exact or 
semantic match, and probe metadata. The acquired observations are encoded into a diagnostic state h_t.
Output: a diagnostic set Gamma_alpha(h_t), a source-sensitive representation, and optionally a correction utility estimate. 
The diagnostic set is calibrated on held-out data so that the true source or failure family is contained with target coverage 
1-alpha.
Optimization target: minimize diagnostic set size, probe cost, and break-aware correction risk subject to diagnostic 
coverage. A compact form is: minimize E[ |Gamma_alpha(h_T)| + lambda C(pi) + eta R_break(pi) ], subject to P(f(x) in 
Gamma_alpha(h_T)) >= 1-alpha.
3. Why This Stands Out for ICLR
The paper should not claim novelty through another hallucination benchmark or another prompt-based correction policy. It 
should claim novelty through a new ML problem: active acquisition of diagnostic evidence for multimodal reliability. This 
connects to active feature acquisition, although the costly features here are not tabular fields. They are behavioral 
measurements obtained by running additional MLLM probes.
It also connects to adaptive test-time compute, although the allocation unit differs. Adaptive reasoning methods decide how  
much reasoning to spend. This paper decides what multimodal evidence to measure. That distinction is central. It makes 
the work more than an efficiency tweak.
It further connects to conformal abstention and risk control, although the output object differs. Abstention returns answer 
versus abstain. The proposed method returns a diagnostic set over plausible failure sources. This is a more appropriate 
interface when the source signals are entangled.
4. Literature Review and Gap Analysis
This literature review should be written as an argument, not a catalog. Each block should end by stating why the previous 
literature does not solve active diagnostic measurement.
Block What the literature establishes Gap for this paperMultimodal hallucination benchmarks POPE studies object hallucination and object priors; HallusionBench stresses entangled language hallucination and visual illusion; AMBER covers existence, attribute, and relation hallucinations without relying on an LLM judge; IllusionBench introduces a large visual-illusion suite with 1,051 images and 5,548 QA pairs; PRE-HAL studies perception and reasoning hallucinations across instances, scenes, and relations [7-11].These benchmarks expose source diversity but do not learn which diagnostic evidence to acquire for a new prediction.
Hallucination detection and mitigation Evidential conflict, visual-token uncertainty, visual contrastive decoding, attention calibration,  and visual-aware sparsification estimate or reduce hallucination using fixed signals or fixed interventions [11,16-18].They mainly ask whether an answer is unreliable  or how to mitigate hallucination, not which source probe should be acquired next.
Abstention and conformal risk control Learnable conformal abstention treats thresholds as adaptive actions and reports improved hallucination AUROC, AUARC, calibration, and coverage behavior for LLMs/VLMs [12].This line optimizes answer-versus-abstain decisions. It does not output calibrated diagnostic sets over failure sources.
Adaptive test-time compute Plan-and-Budget and adaptive test-time compute surveys show that fixed inference budgets can overthink easy inputs and underthink hard ones [13,14].They allocate reasoning tokens or reasoning traces. We allocate multimodal diagnostic probes.
Active feature acquisition Active feature acquisition studies instance-wise sequential selection of costly measurements. Recent surveys and stochastic-encoding methods frame feature acquisition as cost-aware  prediction [15,19].This gives the formal ML bridge. Our features are behavioral probes whose values are generated by MLLM forward passes, not static tabular variables.
A strict ICLR reviewer should see a clean gap: existing work either exposes heterogeneous failures, detects unreliability 
with fixed evidence, optimizes abstention thresholds, allocates reasoning budget, or acquires costly features in non-MLLM 
settings. The proposed work is the first to treat multimodal reliability as calibrated active diagnostic measurement under 
probe cost.
5. Methodology Directions
The recommended method is Active Diagnostic Sets (ADS). It should have four components: a full-evidence teacher, a 
sequential diagnostic state, a value-of-information probe policy, and a conformal diagnostic-set head. Correction utility 
should be a secondary downstream test, not a primary contribution.
Component Technical design Why it mattersFull-evidence teacher Run the complete source-probe suite on training/calibration data. Store source signatures, failure-family labels, answer flips, confidence shifts, and correction outcomes.Protects the role of full probing as the audit-time reference and avoids the framing that the full probe suite is useless.
Sequential diagnostic state Encode clean features and acquired probe observations into h_t using a GRU or small transformer over evidence tokens.Makes the method adaptive and instance-specific rather than a fixed probe order.
Value-of-information policy Estimate VOI(a | h_t) = expected reduction in diagnostic loss or diagnostic-set size minus probe cost. Choose the highest-value probe or stop.This is the main ML contribution. It learns which evidence to acquire, not merely how to classify.
Conformal diagnostic set Predict scores for source/failure states and calibrate Gamma_alpha(h_t) to cover the true state with target coverage.Avoids overclaiming clean source attribution and  matches the entangled nature of multimodal failures.Break-aware utility head Estimate correction utility with fix, break, and cost terms. Use it only after diagnostic evidence is acquired.Shows actionability while avoiding the claim that routing is a general mitigation method.
The value-of-information objective can be implemented in two stages. First, learn a diagnostic predictor and diagnostic-set 
head from full-probe records. Second, label each state-probe pair by the reduction in validation loss or conformal set size 
obtained after acquiring that probe. A lightweight policy then learns to predict this value and choose probes under a budget.
The method should be budget-conditioned. The remaining budget B_t is part of the policy input. A single learned policy 
should produce the full frontier for budgets such as 1, 2, 3, 4, and full probing. This makes the main result visually and 
scientifically stronger.
The representation should preserve entanglement. We should not impose orthogonality or independent source heads as a 
default. Instead, use independent heads only as an ablation. The main representation should allow visual, language-prior, 
and alignment evidence to move together.
6. Expected Result Pattern for a 10-Target Submission
The paper needs one dominant figure and several supporting tables. The dominant figure is the cost-diagnostic frontier: 
diagnostic quality, calibrated set size, or coverage-adjusted efficiency versus average forward passes. The expected 
pattern should be prepared before experiments so that the team knows what counts as a credible ICLR outcome.
Result Target pattern Why it mattersCost-diagnostic frontier Active Diagnostic Sets approach the full-probe teacher using substantially fewer passes; scalar confidence and clean-only predictors lag; fixed probe orders win only in source-specific slices.This is the main ICLR figure.
Set size under coverage At 90% or 95% diagnostic coverage, active probing yields smaller diagnostic sets than clean-only or fixed-probe baselines and approaches full probing.This makes the diagnostic-set interface concrete.
Adaptive probe behavior Blank probes are selected for object-prior or language-prior risk; visual probes for visual insufficiency; relation/grounding probes for relation and binding stress; multiple probes for mixed cases.Shows that the policy is not learning a fixed order.
Leave-model-out Train on several MLLMs and test on a held-out MLLM. ADS should remain above scalar, clean-only, random, and fixed policies at matched cost.This is mandatory for ICLR strength.
Held-out source shift IllusionBench, PRE-HAL, and/or VIA-Bench are harder. Transfer can be imperfect, but active probing should reduce set size or improve diagnosis relative to clean-only baselines.Provides honest generalization evidence.
Break-aware action utility ADS reduces break-aware regret or harmful correction breakage in high-risk slices, without claiming universal accuracy gains.Supports actionability as secondary evidence.
Non-negotiable go/no-go. Proceed only if adaptive probing beats random acquisition at matched budget, beats at least 
two fixed probe orders on the average frontier, clean-only prediction does not match full probing, and leave-model-out 
remains above scalar and random baselines.

Ablations should include: no stop action, no value-of-information head, no source reconstruction loss, no confidence-shift 
features, no answer-flip features, forced independent source heads, black-box versus white-box features, no relation probe,  
no conformal calibration, and one-pass distillation. These ablations map directly to reviewer objections.
Generalization tests should be prioritized in this order: leave-model-out, held-out source-shift datasets, held-out probe 
severity, leave-dataset-out, and optional proprietary-model stress. Leave-model-out is the most important for ICLR because  
it tests whether the acquisition policy captures model-agnostic diagnostic structure rather than idiosyncrasies of one MLLM.

13. Hard Go/No-Go Criteria
1.ADS beats random acquisition at the same budget.
2.ADS beats at least two fixed probe orders on the average cost-diagnostic frontier.
3.Clean-only prediction does not match full probing.
4.Diagnostic sets shrink meaningfully after adaptive probing while maintaining target coverage.
5.Leave-model-out ADS remains above scalar confidence and clean-only baselines.
6.Full probing remains the best upper-bound reference for hard or mixed-source cases.
7.The paper can be explained without relying on HalluPrism as prior knowledge.
If the first four criteria fail by Week 8, the paper should not be pushed as an ICLR 10-target submission. If leave-model-out 
fails by Week 9, the paper may still be interesting but should be reframed or redirected.
